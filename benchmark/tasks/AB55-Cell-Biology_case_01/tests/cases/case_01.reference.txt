The supplied observations contradict the simple claim that the60nM Vδ1 binder must produce the weakest degranulation in these experiments: the source describes similar CD107a responses across a panel spanning below1 to60nM Vδ1 affinity. However, this does not establish that affinity never matters. The paired PD-L1 affinities, format, epitope, receptor density and assay window can all affect the observed relationship, and a restricted panel cannot define a universal affinity rule. The report of no statistically significant difference in A375 lysis also does not prove biological equivalence; that would require a predefined equivalence margin, adequate precision and an appropriate analysis. CD107a is an effector degranulation marker, whereas live-GFP target loss is a distinct24h cytotoxicity readout. The corrected conclusion is that the tested constructs show similar functional responses under the specified conditions despite different measured Vδ1 affinities. One immediate check is a matched-format affinity series with fixed PD-L1 arm and controlled target density, which tests affinity dependence. A second is a powered equivalence or dose-response comparison of both CD107a and target lysis, which tests whether the apparent similarity is biologically meaningful and whether the endpoints agree.
