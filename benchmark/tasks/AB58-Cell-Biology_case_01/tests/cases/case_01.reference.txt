The comparisons do not establish greater intrinsic potency perVδ1 cell in the PBMC preparation. The40:1 ratio uses total PBMCs as its denominator, whereas the skin-cell assay uses an enriched Vδ1 effector preparation at1:1. In the reported PBMC mixture, only8.6% of cells are Vδ1 and83% are αβT cells; changing the total-cell ratio therefore changes both potential Vδ1 input and the number of other cells. The seven-day priming history also changes activation state and composition, so it cannot be treated as interchangeable with the skin-derived-cell assay. All PBMC-mediated lysis cannot be assigned exclusively toVδ1 cells from an aggregate mixed-cell readout. αβT cells, accessory-cell interactions and priming-dependent changes remain alternative or contributing explanations. The corrected conclusion is that the source demonstrates activity in two distinct effector contexts, without a matched estimate of perVδ1-cell potency or exclusive causal attribution. A first discriminating experiment is to compare purified Vδ1 cells from both preparations at matched viable Vδ1:tumor ratios and matched activation histories. A second is selective Vδ1 depletion and add-back in the PBMC mixture, paired with αβT-cell or accessory-cell controls and live target-cell counts. These tests separate cell-intrinsic activity from mixture and priming effects.
