The ABS-201 study used a single IV dose of 100 or 300 mg/kg in cynomolgus monkeys, with four animals per cohort, two male and two female. AUCinf rose from 426,000 to 1,540,000 h·µg/mL: approximately 3.62-fold for a threefold dose. Reported clearance decreased from 0.235 to 0.195 mL/h/kg. In contrast, AUC0–336 rose from 206,000 to 626,000 h·µg/mL, approximately 3.04-fold. The finite-window observations are therefore much closer to dose proportionality than the extrapolated totals.

However, extrapolation contributes substantially to both AUCinf estimates. A modest apparent change in total exposure does not establish target saturation or another specific mechanism. Small cohorts, sex effects, terminal-slope uncertainty, assay behavior and immunogenicity remain alternatives. The source uses ABS-201 in this table and AB001 elsewhere; identities must be mapped explicitly rather than silently merged. These data also do not establish tissue exposure or a clinical dosing rule.

First, extend or verify terminal sampling and fit quality to assess the extrapolated AUC contribution. Second, add intermediate doses and inspect individual profiles with ADA and assay controls. These checks distinguish reproducible nonlinear disposition from cohort or analysis effects.
