The observed terminal beta-phase half-lives were 1.0 hour for the HER2 monomer 2Rs15D, 1.3 hours for the unmodified trimer 3x2Rs15D, and 5.8 hours for the negatively charged trimer 3x(-)2Rs15D. Thus the negative trimer persisted longest in this experiment. All retained HER2 binding, although engineering changed the binding KD by approximately 1.8-fold relative to the unmodified trimer.

However, the experiment used six-week-old female BALB/c mice and 100 µg intraperitoneal dosing, with terminal sampling from 30 minutes to 24 hours and selected trimer observations to 48 hours. These are assay-derived beta-phase estimates, not a direct measurement of IV clearance. Absorption after IP administration, construct-dependent immunoassay behavior and sampling may contribute. The tested negative trimer combines surface changes with multimeric architecture; the result cannot establish a universal charge-only mechanism or predict clinical exposure.

First, compare charge-engineered and unmodified monomers and trimers in a matched factorial design to isolate charge and valency contributions. Second, obtain matched IV profiles using intact-analyte detection and the same sampling budget. These checks separate elimination from absorption and detect whether apparent persistence depends on assay recognition or degradation.
