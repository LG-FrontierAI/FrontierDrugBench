DSS and TNBS colitis experiments differ in model induction, treatment regimens and biological context. In the reported TNBS experiment, both the model group and the combination group, each with five animals, had died by day 5. The DSS experiment reported all animals alive at day 9. These are model-specific observations; they cannot be combined into a single cross-model efficacy ranking or interpreted as proof of one toxic mechanism.

However, later weight, cytokine or histology measurements among survivors are conditional on survival. Death is not a normal missing PD value. Removing animals who died, or imputing their subsequent measurements as normal, can create an apparent treatment benefit through survivor selection. Likewise, a combination regimen cannot be attributed to a single antibody without appropriate matched arms, dosing and exposure information.

First, analyze prespecified survival or a joint survival/PD endpoint for all randomized animals, retaining deaths and reasons for missingness. Second, verify surrogate identity, dose schedule, single-agent controls and exposure within each model. These checks distinguish reproducible model-specific efficacy or harm from selection, regimen differences and unequal exposure. No exact figure coordinates or mortality rates beyond the supplied narrative should be invented.
