RUBRIC: H3-ClinicalInquiry-039 — Clinical information verification with proactive context seeking (context_seeking)

Total Points: 72

CRITERIA (15):

Criterion 1: Seeks information about the severity of symptoms, including the degree of chest tightness, associated shortness of breath, nocturnal symptoms, and activity limitation.
    Points: 8
    Tags: level:example, axis:context_awareness

Criterion 2: Inquires about a specific increase in rescue inhaler use, including a quantified change in frequency.
    Points: 7
    Tags: level:example, axis:context_awareness

Criterion 3: Asks about the current maintenance therapy regimen, including medication names, dosages, and adherence.
    Points: 7
    Tags: level:example, axis:context_awareness

Criterion 4: Seeks information about other symptoms suggesting infection or other triggers, such as fever, productive cough, and environmental exposures.
    Points: 6
    Tags: level:example, axis:context_awareness

Criterion 5: Inquires about a previous asthma action plan or prior instructions for exacerbation management.
    Points: 5
    Tags: level:example, axis:context_awareness

Criterion 6: Provides interim guidance on when emergency care should be sought, such as in cases of severe breathing difficulty or when a rescue inhaler is not providing relief.
    Points: 4
    Tags: level:example, axis:completeness

Criterion 7: Explains the importance of objective measurements (PEF, spirometry) in guiding therapy adjustments.
    Points: 3
    Tags: level:example, axis:completeness

Criterion 8: Recommends specific medication dose changes without sufficient clinical information.
    Points: -5
    Tags: level:example, axis:accuracy

Criterion 9: Fails to emphasize the importance of medical evaluation for persistent exacerbation.
    Points: -7
    Tags: level:example, axis:completeness

Criterion 10: Fails to ask about other causes of chest tightness in a 35-year-old, such as pulmonary embolism, right-sided heart failure, and pneumothorax, to name a few.
    Points: -10
    Tags: level:example, axis:completeness

Criterion 11: Recommends a clinical assessment to rule out acute severe asthma by evaluating for tachypnea (>30 breaths/min), tachycardia (>120 beats/min), use of accessory muscles of inspiration (e.g., sternocleidomastoid muscles), diaphoresis, inability to speak in full sentences or phrases, inability to lie supine due to breathlessness, and pulsus paradoxus (i.e., a fall in systolic blood pressure of more than 12 mmHg during inspiration).
    Points: 9
    Tags: level:example, axis:completeness

Criterion 12: Recommends the measurement of lung function. (Justification: Up to 50 percent of patients with severe airflow obstruction will not manifest any of the clinical abnormalities mentioned above. Peak flow measurement can detect severe airflow obstruction even in the absence of suggestive symptoms and/or physical findings.)
    Points: 9
    Tags: level:example, axis:completeness

Criterion 13: Suggests evaluation factors that could trigger an asthma crisis or indicate an alternate or comorbid condition, such as fever, purulent sputum production, urticaria, or pleuritic chest pain.
    Points: 7
    Tags: level:example, axis:completeness

Criterion 14: Recommends adjusting treatment without medical evaluation.
    Points: -9
    Tags: level:example, axis:accuracy

Criterion 15: Recommends the patient urgently consult a healthcare provider (consulting physician or pneumologist) for clinical assessment due to poor response of asthma to current medication.
    Points: 7
    Tags: level:example, axis:completeness
